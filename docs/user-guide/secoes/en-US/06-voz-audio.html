Record, listen to and send voice messages, and control playback without leaving the chat.

Record

ShortcutAction
Ctrl+RRecord voice message
Ctrl+Shift+PPause and resume the recording
Ctrl+PListen to what was recorded while the recording is paused
Ctrl+Shift+DDiscard the recording
Ctrl+Shift+HRecord microphone and computer audio
  • Voice message recording channels: There is one record button. In Settings > Audio, "Voice message recording channels" offers "Automatic (use the device's channels)" (the default), "Mono (mix left and right)" and "Stereo (when available)". Automatic and Stereo keep two channels when the device supports them and use mono otherwise. Mono averages left and right into one channel: use it for a microphone connected only to INPUT 1 or INPUT 2 of an audio interface. A single active input is quieter after averaging, and no automatic gain is applied. In Automatic, a recording with one side completely silent goes out in mono, taken from the side with sound. The choice is fixed when recording starts and also applies to the paused preview. It does not affect calls or microphone + computer audio recording. Stereo voice messages also play on iPhone.
  • Microphone + computer audio: records in a single message your voice and what the computer plays (a video, music, the screen reader). During the recording, two sliders adjust the volume of the other sounds and of NVDA (without NVDA, the second one is disabled); they only apply to the recording and are kept per account. Before starting, WinZapp warns that everything that plays will go into the recording, with "Don't show again" (the option "Warn before recording computer audio" brings the warning back). If WinZapp stalls for a second or two (a slow or busy computer), the sound of that moment is skipped and the recording goes on. The recording is sent as a voice message, in stereo when both channels carry sound.
  • Recording announcements can be silenced; the screen reader does not start saying "Send" before being interrupted.
  • Microphone noise reduction: with "Enable microphone noise reduction" checked in Settings > Audio, open the noise reducer settings and choose the engine (neural or basic), when to process (while recording or when sending) and what happens if neural reduction fails. The neural model is downloaded once from the noise reducer settings.
  • The microphone and speaker used are in Settings > Audio.
  • With Neonize, the voice message is also delivered to contacts on Android.
  • If the computer's file-type library (libmagic) fails to load, Neonize recognizes the type from the file itself and the voice message goes out normally.
  • Mono voice messages are recorded at 128 kbps by default and stereo ones at 128 kbps. In Settings > Audio, "Voice message bitrate (kbps)" (mono) and "Stereo voice message bitrate (kbps)" let you choose from 32 to 256 kbps.

Listen

  • On voice and audio messages, Enter or Space play and pause. In Settings > Audio you can tell voice messages apart from audio files.
  • Speed: Alt+, slows down and Alt+. speeds up. The default speed is set in Settings > Audio > Audio playback.
  • Forward and back: Shift+Right/Left Arrow moves 5 seconds; Shift+Page Down/Up moves 1 minute; Shift+Home and Shift+End go to the start and the end of what is playing.
  • The following voice messages play in sequence, unless you clear "Automatically play voice messages in sequence" in Settings > User interface. During the sequence, the focused row waits to update its state, so the screen reader does not read over the audio.
  • Ctrl+Alt+Shift+P pauses or resumes the audio that is playing, from any panel.
  • If the output device changes (headphones unplugged, for example), WinZapp switches to the default device and plays the message the first time.
  • If the sound card is not available (for example, when the computer wakes up), WinZapp tries to restore the audio output when a playback fails; if it still cannot play, it tells you through the screen reader.
  • In Settings, there is an option not to mark audio as played.

Switch the audio device on the spot

Ctrl+Alt+Shift+H opens the list of playback devices and Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G the list of recording devices, numbered 1 to 0: press the number to switch right away, or use the arrows and Enter. The switch applies to sounds, voice messages and calls (a call in progress changes without dropping), for all open accounts, until you close WinZapp. The devices chosen in Settings do not change.

Enable microphone noise reduction in Settings > Audio. Open the noise reducer settings to download the model once, choose the processor (CPU, CUDA 12/13 on Windows, or CoreML on Mac) and manage its required components. Processing stays on your computer. Choose the engine, processing moment and failure action in the same window:

Noise reduction engine
Neural / Basic (noise gate)
Process voice messages
When sending / While recording
When noise reduction fails
Send with basic noise reduction (default) / Keep the voice message pending to try sending it again

Keep the default send-time processing, or choose recording-time processing to work in the background as you record. Recording-time processing is available only with CPU or the in-process CoreML path. Choosing CUDA disables that choice and uses send-time processing; there is no CUDA recording-time processing. CoreML recording-time processing has not been validated in real use. If neural processing fails, the default is to send with basic reduction. Choose the pending option to keep the message instead: retry manually from its context menu, or explicitly choose "Send without noise reduction".

Default device and recordings

Sounds and calls set to "system default" now follow Windows' default playback device: when you plug in a headset that Windows makes the default, WinZapp's sounds and the call audio move to it, as other programs do.

While you record audio (a voice message or microphone plus computer audio), the recurring background sync, the history walk and the media downloads pause, as they already did during a call, so the recording is not cut into pieces by a busy computer.
